How to Copy Text from an Image or Screenshot on iPhone

Text in a screenshot, a paper handout, a menu or a sign: copying it instead of retyping saves a lot of time. Here is what iPhone can do on its own, and when an OCR app is the better tool.

Start with iPhone's built-in Live Text

iPhone can already recognize text in photos and screenshots with Live Text. Open an image in Photos, touch and hold the text, select it and tap Copy. You can also point the Camera at text and tap the text icon in the corner.

If you only need to copy one short passage, that's all you need. No app required.

When an OCR app helps

  • You want all the text at once: Live Text needs you to drag a selection. With long or small text, selecting takes time.
  • You have many screenshots: Combine several screenshots or photos into one piece of text.
  • You need a table as a table: Paste receipts, schedules or price lists into a spreadsheet with rows and columns intact.
  • Line breaks from books are messy: Join lines broken mid-sentence into one paragraph.
  • You want to find it later: Keep the text and search it later, so you can throw the paper away.
  • You want a PDF: Scan paper documents into searchable PDFs.

What OCR Copy does

  • Turn screenshots and photos into text straight from the Share menu, without opening the app.
  • Read several images at once and choose to combine them or keep them separate.
  • Export tables as CSV with rows and columns, and join broken lines into one paragraph.
  • Shaky lines are highlighted. On supported iPhones, on-device AI suggests fixes and only applies the ones you confirm.
  • Hold your iPhone over a document to capture it automatically, straighten it, and save a searchable PDF.
  • Keep what you read in a searchable library you can lock with Face ID.
  • Images and text are processed and stored on your device and never sent to a server.
  • Free for 5 reads a day. Pro (subscription) removes the daily limit and adds translation.
