How to Measure Your Child's Foot Size at Home

Kids' feet grow quietly, and a shoe that fit in spring can be tight by fall. You don't need a store visit or a special device: a sheet of paper and a pen are enough. Here is how to measure, and what to look for when you pick the next pair.

What you need

  • A sheet of Letter (8.5 × 11 in) or A4 paper
  • A dark pen or marker
  • A ruler or tape measure
  • A hard floor (not carpet)

Measure in 5 steps

  1. Put the paper on a hard floor. Tape it down so it doesn't slide.
  2. Have your child stand on it. Measure with their weight on the foot. If they sit and lift the foot, it measures smaller than it really is. Line the foot up with the long edge of the paper.
  3. Trace around the foot. Hold the pen as upright as you can and trace all the way around without gaps. Take extra care at the heel, the toes and the widest points on each side.
  4. Measure the length. Remove the foot and measure from the back of the heel to the tip of the longest toe. On some kids the second toe is longer than the big toe, so use whichever is longest.
  5. Measure the other foot. Feet often differ a little. Measure both and use the larger one.

A tilted pen or a line that drifts away from the foot adds a few millimeters. With little ones it helps to have two people: one to hold, one to trace.

Choosing shoes after you measure

  • Leave about 10–15 mm (3/8–5/8 in) of toe room in front of the longest toe, as the American Podiatric Medical Association suggests.
  • Fit the larger foot. If the two feet differ, buy with the larger one in mind.
  • Check the inside, not just the label. The same labeled size can fit differently between brands. If the insole comes out, have your child stand on it and look at the space in front of the toes.
  • Too big isn't safe either. In a loose shoe the foot slides around, which makes it harder to stay steady.

These are general guides. Always finish with a try-on: check the toes and make sure the heel doesn't slip.

How often to re-measure

A column by the Japanese Society for Foot Care and Podiatric Medicine suggests:

  • Up to age 3: every 3 months
  • Up to age 6: every 4 months
  • Age 7 and up: every 6 months

Japan's Foot Health Association notes that children's feet grow by roughly 10 mm a year, so shoes usually need replacing at least twice a year.

Sources
