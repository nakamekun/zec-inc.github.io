Overview

Age Profile is a Japanese profile book for iPhone that keeps the birthdays, anniversaries, and age milestones of your family, your oshi (favorite idols or artists), and friends in one place. Open the app and the Today screen shows today's calendar notes (the day's zodiac sign and rokuyo), whose birthday, anniversary, or memorial day it is today, and what is coming up next.

Each profile shows full age, kazoedoshi (traditional East Asian age counting), birth date in wareki (Japanese era calendar: Meiji, Taisho, Showa, Heisei, Reiwa), Chinese zodiac animal, star sign, common yakudoshi (traditionally inauspicious years) guidance, and longevity celebrations such as kanreki (60th) and koki (70th). The list switches between All, Family, Oshi, and Friends. An oshi can be a person or a group: for a group, the app counts down to the next anniversary of its formation or debut instead of a birthday, and a person's birthday can be saved with just the month and day. Family members who have passed away can stay in the book too, with their age at death (full and kazoedoshi), how old they would be today, the next memorial day, and memorial-service years such as the first and third anniversaries.

Add any number of anniversaries, such as debut dates, formation dates, wedding anniversaries, or the day you met, and see how many days have passed and how many remain until the next 100-day milestone. Birthday, longevity, memorial, and anniversary notifications can be turned on or off per person, and an oshi's birthday or anniversary can be shared as a washi-paper card image. The Birthdays view lists the coming year's birthdays and anniversaries in date order. A built-in converter switches dates between the Western calendar and wareki across the five modern eras, and can read non-existent era dates such as Heisei 32 or Showa 100 as the correct Western year and era. Home Screen and Lock Screen widgets are included.

Profiles, notes, and notification settings are stored on your device: no account, no ads, and profiles are never sent to external servers automatically. Yakudoshi, longevity, and memorial years are shown as common guidance, and rokuyo and zodiac compatibility as traditional calendar lore. The app makes no claims about fortune, misfortune, or health, and includes no information about celebrities.

How to Use

  1. Add family members, oshi (a person or a group), and friends with their birthdays or anniversaries
  2. Open the Today screen to see today's calendar notes and whose birthday, anniversary, or memorial day it is
  3. Open a profile to see age, wareki, zodiac, yakudoshi, and longevity or memorial-year guidance at a glance
  4. Add anniversaries such as a debut or wedding date and count the days to the next milestone
  5. Turn on notifications per person so you do not miss birthdays, anniversaries, or memorial days
  6. Add a Home Screen or Lock Screen widget, and use the converter to switch dates between the Western calendar and wareki

FAQ

Is the app free?

Yes, it is free to download and use. The free version stores up to 2 family members, 1 oshi, and 1 friend. A one-time Plus purchase (not a subscription) removes these limits. Check the App Store listing for current pricing.

Where is my data stored?

Everything is stored locally on your device. Profiles are not sent to external servers automatically, there is no account, and the app uses no ads, tracking, or analytics SDKs.

What does the Today screen show?

Today's calendar notes (the day's zodiac sign and rokuyo), whose birthday, anniversary, or memorial day it is today, and what is coming up next.

Can I save my oshi or a group?

Yes. Choose Oshi and then a person or a group. For a person, you can save the birthday with only the month and day. For a group, the app counts down to the next anniversary of its formation or debut. The app does not include any celebrity information, so you enter it yourself.

Can I keep family members who have passed away?

Yes. The app shows their age at death (full and kazoedoshi), how old they would be today, the next memorial day, and memorial-service years such as the first and third anniversaries. How these years are counted can differ by religious tradition and region, so treat them as general guidance.

What is wareki and which eras does the app support?

Wareki is the Japanese era calendar used on official documents. The app converts dates across the five modern eras — Meiji, Taisho, Showa, Heisei, and Reiwa — correctly handles the dates where eras change, and can read non-existent notations such as Heisei 32 or Showa 100 as the right Western year and era.

What are yakudoshi and kazoedoshi?

Yakudoshi are years traditionally considered inauspicious in Japan, which differ for men and women. Kazoedoshi is the traditional age counting where you are 1 at birth. The app shows both as common cultural guidance, without any claims about fortune or misfortune.

Can it remind me about birthdays and anniversaries?

Yes. You can turn notifications for birthdays, longevity celebrations, memorial days and memorial-service years, and anniversaries on or off for each person.

Does it have a widget?

Yes. Home Screen and Lock Screen widgets are available.

Is this a fortune-telling app?

No. Yakudoshi, longevity, and memorial years are shown as widely used reference points, and rokuyo and zodiac compatibility are explained as traditional calendar lore without scores. The app makes no statements about luck, misfortune, health, or religious effect.
